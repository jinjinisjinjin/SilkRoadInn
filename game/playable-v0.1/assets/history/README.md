# Historical-note image provenance and reuse

These three images are educational references, not pictures of the fictional
Liushayi inn or its characters. Each image is shown with a source/credit link in
the game. The WebP assets were resized proportionally and compressed; they were
not cropped, retouched, or otherwise altered.

## `jiaohe-well.webp`

- Depicts: a surviving water well at the Jiaohe city ruins (交河故城水井), Turpan.
  This photograph does **not** document the game's fictional well or establish
  the date of the photographed structure.

## `tang-silk-textile.webp`

- Object: *Textile with floral medallions* (唐 紫地團花紋「正倉院」錦), China, Tang dynasty, 8th century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Seymour Fund, 1965, accession 65.213.2. Maker not named on the object page.
- Object page: <https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/52098>
- Image download (main view): <https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/api/collection/v1/iiif/52098/163914/main-image>
- License: [The Met Open Access, CC0](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources); the object page explicitly marks the image "Public Domain." Attribution is not required by CC0, but The Met requests a collection citation for educational use.
- Processing: 1200 × 1110 JPEG → 1200 × 1110 WebP, quality 78; no crop.
- Interpretation limit: this is an 8th-century Tang silk textile, **not** the 742 Xizhou horse-price record, nor evidence that this particular textile was used as payment.

## `tang-dancer-pot.webp`

- Object: *Ewer with dancing figures* (唐 長沙窯舞人紋壺), China, Tang dynasty, about 9th century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Purchase, Friends of Asian Art Gifts, 1986, accession 1986.113. Maker not named on the object page.
- Object page: <https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/42395>
- Image download (second view, dancer more visible): <https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/api/collection/v1/iiif/42395/1797404/main-image>
- License: [The Met Open Access, CC0](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources); the object page explicitly marks the image "Public Domain." Attribution is not required by CC0, but The Met requests a collection citation for educational use.
- Processing: 1200 × 901 JPEG → 1200 × 901 WebP, quality 78; no crop.
- Interpretation limit: The Met says the figure **may** represent someone from South/Southeast/Central Asia and that similar ceramic dancers often represent Sogdians. This object is **not** a painting from Mogao Cave 220, and the dancer's identity must not be stated as certain.

The Met's [image and data FAQ](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/frequently-asked-questions-image-and-data-resources) explains that its Open Access images may be used commercially or non-commercially without permission and gives a suggested attribution format.
